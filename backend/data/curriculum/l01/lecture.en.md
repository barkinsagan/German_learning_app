# Lesson 1 — Countries, Alphabet

In this first lesson you'll learn how to introduce yourself, talk about where you come from, spell using the German alphabet, and ask your first questions using **W-Fragen** (wh-questions).

**Learning goals:**

- Greet people and introduce yourself
- Say where you come from and where you live
- Pronounce and spell the German alphabet
- Ask simple questions with *wie*, *woher*, *wo*
- Conjugate verbs in the singular (*ich*, *du*, *er/sie/es*)

---

## 1. Greetings and Introductions

German has both **formal** and **informal** greetings. Choose the right form for the situation.

### Informal (friends, family, young people)

- **Hallo!** — Hello!
- **Hi!** — Hi!
- **Tschüss!** — Bye!
- **Bis bald!** — See you soon!

### Formal (at work, with strangers, in shops)

- **Guten Morgen!** — Good morning! *(until about 11 am)*
- **Guten Tag!** — Good day! *(during the day)*
- **Guten Abend!** — Good evening! *(from about 6 pm)*
- **Auf Wiedersehen!** — Goodbye!

### Introducing yourself

| German | English |
| --- | --- |
| Ich heiße Anna. | My name is Anna. |
| Mein Name ist Anna Müller. | My name is Anna Müller. |
| Ich bin Anna. | I am Anna. |
| Wie heißen Sie? | What is your name? *(formal)* |
| Wie heißt du? | What is your name? *(informal)* |

> **Remember:** *Sie* (formal "you") is always capitalized. *du* (informal "you") is lowercase.

## 2. Vocabulary: Countries (Länder)

### German-speaking countries

- **Deutschland** — Germany 🇩🇪
- **Österreich** — Austria 🇦🇹
- **die Schweiz** — Switzerland 🇨🇭
- **Liechtenstein** — Liechtenstein 🇱🇮

### Important countries around the world

- **die Türkei** — Turkey
- **Frankreich** — France
- **Italien** — Italy
- **Spanien** — Spain
- **Polen** — Poland
- **Russland** — Russia
- **die USA** — the USA
- **China** — China
- **Japan** — Japan
- **Brasilien** — Brazil

### Talking about origin

The most important question: **Woher kommst du?** / **Woher kommen Sie?** — *Where are you from?*

The answer: **Ich komme aus …** — *I come from …*

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Woher kommst du? | Ich komme aus Deutschland. — I'm from Germany. |
| Woher kommen Sie? | Ich komme aus Österreich. — I'm from Austria. |
| Wo wohnst du? | Ich wohne in Berlin. — I live in Berlin. |
| Wo wohnen Sie? | Ich wohne in Wien. — I live in Vienna. |

### Watch out: articles with country names

Most countries have **no article**. But a few take *die* or *der*:

- **die Schweiz** *(Switzerland)*, **die Türkei** *(Turkey)*, **die USA** *(plural!)*
- **der Iran**, **der Irak**, **der Sudan**

With these countries you need **aus + the article in the dative case**:

- Ich komme **aus der** Schweiz. — I come from Switzerland.
- Ich komme **aus der** Türkei. — I come from Turkey.
- Ich komme **aus den** USA. — I come from the USA.
- Ich komme **aus dem** Iran. — I come from Iran.

With countries that have no article, just use **aus**:

- Ich komme **aus** Deutschland.
- Ich komme **aus** Polen.

## 3. Vocabulary: The German Alphabet

The German alphabet has **the same 26 letters** as English — plus four extra letters: **Ä, Ö, Ü, ß**.

### Letter pronunciation

| Letter | Sounds like | Letter | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | *ah* | N | *en* |
| B | *beh* | O | *oh* |
| C | *tseh* | P | *peh* |
| D | *deh* | Q | *koo* |
| E | *eh* | R | *err* |
| F | *eff* | S | *ess* |
| G | *geh* | T | *teh* |
| H | *hah* | U | *oo* |
| I | *ee* | V | *fow* (like "cow") |
| J | *yot* | W | *veh* |
| K | *kah* | X | *iks* |
| L | *ell* | Y | *üpsilon* |
| M | *emm* | Z | *tsett* |

### The umlauts and ß

- **Ä, ä** — like the *a* in English "cat" but a bit longer
- **Ö, ö** — round your lips as if to say *o*, but say *e*
- **Ü, ü** — round your lips as if to say *oo*, but say *ee*
- **ß** — called *eszett* or *sharp s*; it sounds exactly like *ss*

> **Tip:** On an English keyboard you can also write umlauts as *ae*, *oe*, *ue* and *ß* as *ss*: *Müller → Mueller*, *groß → gross*.

### Spelling things out

When giving your name on the phone, Germans often **spell it out**:

> *Mein Name ist Schmidt. S-C-H-M-I-D-T.*

Useful question: **Wie schreibt man das?** — *How do you write that?*

Answer: **Das schreibt man so: …** + spell it letter by letter.

## 4. Grammar: Verb Conjugation in the Singular

A German verb has a **stem** and an **ending**. The ending changes depending on the subject (who is doing the action).

**Infinitive:** kommen *(to come)* → **stem:** komm- + **ending:** -en

### Regular verbs in the singular

| Person | Ending | kommen *(come)* | wohnen *(live)* | heißen *(be called)* | lernen *(learn)* |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ich *(I)* | **-e** | komme | wohne | heiße | lerne |
| du *(you, informal)* | **-st** | kommst | wohnst | heißt* | lernst |
| er/sie/es *(he/she/it)* | **-t** | kommt | wohnt | heißt | lernt |

> *When the stem ends in *-s*, *-ß*, *-z*, or *-x*, the *du* form takes only *-t* (not *-st*): du heißt, du tanzt.

### The verb *sein* (to be) — irregular

| Person | Form |
| --- | --- |
| ich | **bin** |
| du | **bist** |
| er/sie/es | **ist** |

Examples:

- Ich **bin** Student. — *I am a student.*
- Du **bist** aus Italien. — *You are from Italy.*
- Er **ist** Lehrer. — *He is a teacher.*

### Example sentences

- Ich **heiße** Maria und ich **komme** aus Spanien. — *My name is Maria and I'm from Spain.*
- Du **wohnst** in Berlin, oder? — *You live in Berlin, right?*
- Er **lernt** Deutsch in München. — *He's learning German in Munich.*
- Sie **kommt** aus der Türkei und sie **wohnt** in Hamburg. — *She's from Turkey and lives in Hamburg.*

> **Important:** *sie* can mean *she* OR *they*. In the singular (*she*) → **kommt**. In the plural (*they*) → **kommen** (you'll learn this in Lesson 2).

## 5. Grammar: W-Questions (W-Fragen)

**W-Fragen** are open questions that begin with a question word. They **cannot** be answered with just *yes* or *no*.

### The most important W-words

| W-word | Meaning | Example question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wie?** | How? / What? | Wie heißt du? — *What's your name?* |
| **Wer?** | Who? | Wer ist das? — *Who is that?* |
| **Was?** | What? | Was lernst du? — *What are you learning?* |
| **Wo?** | Where? | Wo wohnst du? — *Where do you live?* |
| **Woher?** | Where from? | Woher kommst du? — *Where are you from?* |
| **Wohin?** | Where to? | Wohin gehst du? — *Where are you going?* |

### Word order

In W-questions, the **verb always comes in position 2** — right after the question word:

| Position 1 | Position 2 | Position 3 | Position 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wie | heißt | du | ? |
| Woher | kommt | sie | ? |
| Wo | wohnen | Sie | ? |
| Was | lernst | du | ? |

### Mini dialogue

> **— Hallo! Wie heißt du?** *(Hi! What's your name?)*
> — Ich heiße Lukas. Und du? *(My name is Lukas. And you?)*
> **— Ich bin Sofia. Woher kommst du, Lukas?** *(I'm Sofia. Where are you from, Lukas?)*
> — Ich komme aus Österreich, aus Wien. Und du? *(I'm from Austria, from Vienna. And you?)*
> **— Ich komme aus Italien. Wo wohnst du jetzt?** *(I'm from Italy. Where do you live now?)*
> — Ich wohne in Berlin. Ich lerne hier Deutsch. *(I live in Berlin. I'm learning German here.)*

## 6. Summary

You can now:

- ✓ Greet people and introduce yourself in German
- ✓ Talk about your home country (*Ich komme aus …*)
- ✓ Say where you live (*Ich wohne in …*)
- ✓ Pronounce the German alphabet, including the umlauts and *ß*
- ✓ Conjugate regular verbs and *sein* in the singular
- ✓ Ask questions with *wie*, *was*, *wo*, *woher*
