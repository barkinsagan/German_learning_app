# Lesson 4 — Numbers 100–1,000,000, Furniture, Adjectives

In this lesson you'll go from **100 to a million**, learn the most important **furniture** for your apartment, and pick up your first **adjectives** for describing things. Grammatically, the focus is on the **definite articles** *der*, *das*, *die* — and the **personal pronouns** *er*, *es*, *sie* that replace those nouns.

**Learning goals:**

- Understand, say, and write large numbers up to 1,000,000
- Name furniture and describe a room
- Describe things with simple adjectives (*groß*, *klein*, *neu*, *alt* …)
- Use the correct definite article (*der*, *das*, *die*)
- Replace nouns with *er*, *es*, *sie* (singular) and *sie* (plural)

---

## 1. Vocabulary: Numbers 100–1,000,000

In Lesson 2 you learned the numbers from 1–100. Now we add the **big numbers**.

### Hundreds

| 100 | (ein)hundert |
| --- | --- |
| 200 | zweihundert |
| 300 | dreihundert |
| 400 | vierhundert |
| 500 | fünfhundert |
| 600 | sechshundert |
| 700 | siebenhundert |
| 800 | achthundert |
| 900 | neunhundert |

> **Note:** Unlike *sechzehn* / *siebzig*, the hundreds keep their full stem: it's **sechs**hundert and **sieben**hundert (not *sechhundert* or *siebhundert*).

### Numbers 101–999

Same as Lesson 2 (ones + und + tens), now with the hundreds in front — and all written **as one word**:

- 101 = **hundert**eins
- 125 = **hundert**fünfundzwanzig
- 247 = **zwei**hundert**sieben**und**vierzig**
- 999 = **neun**hundert**neun**und**neunzig**

> **Watch out:** even in large numbers, the ones-tens combination is still **backwards**: *sieben**und**vierzig*. That rule always applies.

### Thousands and beyond

| 1,000 | (ein)tausend |
| --- | --- |
| 2,000 | zweitausend |
| 10,000 | zehntausend |
| 100,000 | hunderttausend |
| 1,000,000 | eine Million |

- 1,500 = **tausend**fünfhundert *(or: **eintausend**fünfhundert)*
- 12,345 = **zwölf**tausend**drei**hundert**fünf**und**vierzig**
- 1,234,567 = **eine Million zwei**hundert**vier**und**dreißig**tausend**fünf**hundert**sieben**und**sechzig**

> **Important:** In German, thousands are separated with a **period** (or a thin space): *1.000* or *1 000* — **not** *1,000*. The comma is the **decimal separator**: *3,14* = *drei Komma eins vier*.

### Years

For years up to **1999**, Germans say them as two-digit pairs:

- 1995 = **neunzehn**hundert**fünf**und**neunzig** *(literally "nineteen-hundred-five-and-ninety")*
- 1848 = **achtzehn**hundert**acht**und**vierzig**

From **2000** onward, the year is said like a normal number:

- 2024 = **zwei**tausend**vier**und**zwanzig**
- 2010 = **zwei**tausend**zehn**

### Prices and shopping

- **der Euro** — euro *(plural: Euro)*
- **der Cent** — cent
- **kostet** — costs
- **billig** — cheap
- **teuer** — expensive
- **kostenlos** / **gratis** — free

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Was kostet das? — *What does that cost?* | Das kostet 29,90 € *(neunundzwanzig Euro neunzig)*. |
| Wie viel kostet die Lampe? — *How much is the lamp?* | Sie kostet 45 €. |
| Ist das teuer? | Nein, das ist billig. |

## 2. Vocabulary: Furniture (Möbel)

### Living room

- **der Tisch** — table
- **der Stuhl** — chair
- **das Sofa** / **die Couch** — sofa
- **der Sessel** — armchair
- **der Fernseher** — TV
- **das Regal** — shelf
- **die Lampe** — lamp
- **der Teppich** — rug, carpet

### Bedroom

- **das Bett** — bed
- **der Schrank** — wardrobe, cupboard
- **der Kleiderschrank** — clothes wardrobe
- **der Nachttisch** — bedside table
- **der Spiegel** — mirror

### Kitchen

- **der Kühlschrank** — fridge
- **der Herd** — stove
- **die Spüle** — sink
- **die Mikrowelle** — microwave
- **die Küche** — kitchen

### Example sentences

- **Das Sofa** ist neu. — *The sofa is new.*
- **Der Tisch** ist aus Holz. — *The table is wooden.*
- **Die Lampe** steht auf dem Nachttisch. — *The lamp is on the bedside table.*
- Wir haben einen Schrank, ein Bett und einen Stuhl. — *We have a wardrobe, a bed, and a chair.*

### Word formation: compound nouns

German loves to **combine nouns**. The last word determines the article and the core meaning.

- **die Nacht** + **der Tisch** = **der Nacht**tisch *(article from Tisch)*
- **die Kleider** + **der Schrank** = **der Kleider**schrank
- **der Kühl-** + **der Schrank** = **der Kühl**schrank
- **das Wohn-** + **das Zimmer** = **das Wohn**zimmer

> **Rule:** The **last word** is the "head" — it sets the **article** and the **basic meaning**. Everything before it just describes it more specifically.

## 3. Vocabulary: Adjectives (basic set)

Adjectives describe things, people, and places. Here are the most important **opposite pairs**:

| Adjective | Opposite |
| --- | --- |
| **groß** *(big)* | **klein** *(small)* |
| **neu** *(new)* | **alt** *(old)* |
| **billig** *(cheap)* | **teuer** *(expensive)* |
| **schön** *(beautiful)* | **hässlich** *(ugly)* |
| **lang** *(long)* | **kurz** *(short)* |
| **breit** *(wide)* | **schmal** *(narrow)* |
| **hoch** *(tall, high)* | **niedrig** *(low)* |
| **schnell** *(fast)* | **langsam** *(slow)* |
| **leicht** *(light/easy)* | **schwer** *(heavy/hard)* |
| **gut** *(good)* | **schlecht** *(bad)* |
| **hell** *(bright)* | **dunkel** *(dark)* |
| **warm** *(warm)* | **kalt** *(cold)* |

### Adjectives as predicate — no ending!

In this lesson you'll use adjectives **after a verb** (*sein*, *werden*, *bleiben*). In this position they take **no ending**:

- **Das Sofa ist neu.** *(no ending)*
- **Der Tisch ist groß.**
- **Die Lampe ist sehr schön.**
- **Die Stühle sind teuer.**

> **Preview:** When the adjective stands **before the noun** (*ein **neues** Sofa*), it takes an ending. That comes much later — in A2.

### Intensifiers

- **sehr** — very
- **wirklich** — really
- **ziemlich** — quite, fairly
- **nicht so** — not so
- **gar nicht** — not at all

Examples:

- Das Bett ist **sehr** alt. — *The bed is very old.*
- Der Schrank ist **ziemlich** groß. — *The wardrobe is quite big.*
- Die Lampe ist **nicht so** teuer. — *The lamp isn't that expensive.*
- Das Sofa ist **wirklich** schön. — *The sofa is really nice.*

## 4. Grammar: The Definite Article *der*, *das*, *die*

Every German noun has a **grammatical gender**: masculine, neuter, or feminine. In the plural, all nouns take the article *die*.

### The three articles in the nominative

| Gender | Article | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **masculine** | **der** | **der** Tisch, **der** Stuhl, **der** Schrank |
| **neuter** | **das** | **das** Bett, **das** Sofa, **das** Regal |
| **feminine** | **die** | **die** Lampe, **die** Küche, **die** Tochter |
| **plural** | **die** | **die** Tische, **die** Betten, **die** Lampen |

### Why do you need the article?

In German, you always learn a noun **together with its article**. The article tells you:

1. The noun's **gender** (you'll need this for pronouns, adjectives, etc.)
2. Which **form** to use in the accusative, dative, etc. *(coming from L6)*

> **Study tip:** Never write a noun without its article. Not *Tisch*, but **der Tisch**. Not *Lampe*, but **die Lampe**.

### Rules of thumb for gender (not 100% reliable)

There are no fully reliable rules, but a few strong **tendencies**:

| Tendency | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| **-er** (person, often a job) → **der** | der Lehrer, der Vater, der Computer |
| **-or** → **der** | der Doktor, der Professor |
| **-e** (often) → **die** | die Lampe, die Tante, die Küche |
| **-ung, -heit, -keit, -ion** → **die** | die Wohnung, die Freiheit, die Information |
| **-chen, -lein** *(diminutive)* → **das** | das Mädchen, das Brötchen |
| **-um** → **das** | das Museum, das Datum |

> **But:** there are many exceptions. *Das* Mädchen is neuter (even though it's a girl!) because the *-chen* diminutive always makes the noun neuter.

### Watch out: „das Mädchen", „das Kind"

Sometimes the grammatical gender **doesn't match** the natural gender:

- **das Mädchen** — the girl *(neuter because of -chen!)*
- **das Kind** — the child *(neuter, regardless of sex)*
- **die Person** — the person *(always feminine)*

### Example sentences

- **Der** Tisch ist neu. **Die** Stühle sind alt.
- **Das** Sofa ist sehr bequem.
- **Die** Lampe ist schön. **Der** Schrank ist groß.
- **Das** Mädchen heißt Anna.

## 5. Grammar: Personal Pronouns *er*, *es*, *sie*

**Personal pronouns** replace a noun so you don't have to repeat it. In German, the pronoun follows the **grammatical gender** — not the meaning.

### The rule

| Article | Pronoun |
| --- | --- |
| **der** | **er** |
| **das** | **es** |
| **die** *(sg.)* | **sie** |
| **die** *(pl.)* | **sie** |

> **Memory trick:** *de**r** → e**r***, *da**s** → e**s***, *di**e** → si**e***. The endings match!

### Examples

- **Der Tisch** ist neu. → **Er** ist neu. — *The table is new. → It's new.*
- **Das Sofa** ist bequem. → **Es** ist bequem.
- **Die Lampe** ist schön. → **Sie** ist schön.
- **Die Stühle** sind alt. → **Sie** sind alt.

### Important: pronouns follow the gender, not the meaning!

- **Das Mädchen** heißt Anna. → **Es** heißt Anna. *(grammatically correct — even though people often say *sie*)*
- **Der Stuhl** steht in der Küche. → **Er** steht in der Küche. *(not *es*!)*
- **Die Tasche** ist groß. → **Sie** ist groß.

### Pronouns in dialogue

> **— Wie findest du das Sofa?** *(How do you like the sofa?)*
> — **Es** ist sehr bequem, aber **es** ist auch teuer. *(It's very comfy, but it's also expensive.)*
> **— Und der Stuhl?** *(And the chair?)*
> — **Er** ist schön, aber **er** ist nicht so bequem. *(It's nice, but not that comfy.)*
> **— Was kostet die Lampe?** *(How much is the lamp?)*
> — **Sie** kostet 45 Euro. Ich finde **sie** sehr schön. *(It costs 45 euros. I think it's really nice.)*

### Overview — all personal pronouns in the nominative

For completeness, together with the forms from L1–L2:

| Person | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | **ich** | **wir** |
| 2nd (informal) | **du** | **ihr** |
| 3rd masculine | **er** | **sie** |
| 3rd neuter | **es** | **sie** |
| 3rd feminine | **sie** | **sie** |
| 2nd (formal) | **Sie** | **Sie** |

## 6. Mini dialogue

> **— Schau mal, das Sofa hier! Wie findest du es?** *(Look, this sofa here! How do you like it?)*
> — **Es** ist wirklich schön. Was kostet **es**? *(It's really nice. How much is it?)*
> **— Es kostet 599 Euro — fünfhundertneunundneunzig.** *(It costs 599 euros — five hundred and ninety-nine.)*
> — Oh, das ist ziemlich teuer. Und der Sessel da? *(Oh, that's pretty expensive. And the armchair over there?)*
> **— Der ist billiger. Er kostet 249 Euro.** *(That one's cheaper. It's 249 euros.)*
> — Aha. Und die Lampe — ist **sie** auch teuer? *(I see. And the lamp — is it expensive too?)*
> **— Nein, sie ist nicht teuer. Sie kostet nur 35 Euro.** *(No, it isn't. It's only 35 euros.)*
> — Gut. Dann nehme ich den Sessel und die Lampe. *(Good. Then I'll take the armchair and the lamp.)*
